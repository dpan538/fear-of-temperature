## Priority and scope

1. Prioritise 1988–1999 (DoE/MAFF/DETR and other already approved predecessor bodies), with 1988–1992 first because the policy series is entirely empty there.
2. Then fill the documented 2000–2009 policy gaps, especially the sparse 2000–2006 interval, using the same catalogue-led method.
3. Do not expand modern data or resume bulk ministerial collection to increase totals. Preserve mixed-text exclusions from previous decisions.
4. Policy publications need not occur every calendar month. The goal is to distinguish missing known publications, incomplete discovery and verified no in-scope publication in a bounded catalogue. Do not invent dates, spread a long-lived policy across months as new publication records, or impose one-policy-per-month quotas.

## Stage 3 — alternative full-text routes and provenance

For each enumerated missing title, prefer its official original or normal official archive, then a verifiable institutional copy of the same document. University/library catalogue access or scans can be a next route when public archives are blocked. Observe source conditions; do not bypass restrictions. If a library request is needed, prepare the precise title/edition/number/pages/holding and request text for Dai; do not send it without instruction.

Record the UK issuing body separately from a foreign institution hosting its copy. Retain original content, retrieved URL, timestamp, file hash and page mapping. Reuse stable identities. Use local OCR as needed and preserve blank pages. A scan with unknown completeness is not automatically a usable complete policy document.

Publication date precision matters: a document known only to a year cannot be arbitrarily assigned to January to fill the monthly chart. Retain year/month/day precision and source evidence. Exclude unresolved-date records from exact monthly counts while reporting their separate availability until the date is established.
