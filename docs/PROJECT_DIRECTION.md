# Current project direction and execution priorities

Updated: 8 October 2026. Governing development brief following Dai's explicit clarifications. It supersedes conflicting acquisition and stage priorities in older handoffs; frozen source filters and historical evidence retain their original meaning.

## Research objective

Study how climate and rising-temperature discourse connects to social emotion, anticipated harm and collective responses across an international English-language record of government, media and public texts. Fear of warming remains the thesis-level question, with shared collective knowledge, memory and culture as an interpretive perspective. It is not a required label for every collected text or the first-stage retrieval target. The main contribution is a thesis about temporal macro feedback. Countries identify contributing evidence; exhaustive national archives and country-by-country findings are not the primary objective or a condition for progress.

- RQ1: Who changes first across government, media and public climate discourse, and in which validated attention or affect-related measures?
- RQ2: What changes around independently dated physical, scientific and policy events?
- RQ3: How can fear and its targets, horizons and attributed causes be interpreted from similarity structures and traceable original passages? Deferred behind RQ1/RQ2 for this phase.

The planned route remains database → source-aware cleaning and passages → Sentence-Transformer similarity/retrieval → role/time measures → CCF/conditional VAR and event ITS where supported. Existing experimental emotion/relation/topic modules are infrastructure, not additional compulsory tasks. This clarification adds no model or analytical framework.

## Staged construct boundary

1. **Current corpus preparation:** preserve independently dated parent texts, source role and provenance within the fixed publication interval. Audit distribution and source quality; clean structural/numerical defects without selecting records by climate relevance, emotion or anticipated harm. High volume is a diagnostic flag, not automatic overcollection or event evidence.
2. **Later analysis and measurement:** perform climate/warming relevance and similarity validation, then examine associations with affective language, risk, future expectations and responsibility. Algorithm/evaluation design can precede that stage, but climate verification is not a present cleaning or progression gate. Keep attention, risk statements, quoted emotion and a speaker's own emotion distinct. Similarity indicates related language, not an emotion diagnosis or validated fear intensity. RQ1/RQ2 may use only measures whose unit, denominator and cross-source validity are established; otherwise report source readiness or descriptive evidence.
3. **Later interpretation:** return from retrieved clusters or passages to original text and provenance. Distinguish fear from worry, anxiety, grief, direct heat danger and non-emotional risk statements; identify holder, target, temporal horizon, quotation and negation before synthesising narratives or assigning responsibility in discourse. Explicit fear is a narrow diagnostic tag, not a prerequisite for inclusion or an early headline outcome. Quantify it only if annotation and validation support that construct.

Temporal ordering describes observed discourse; it does not by itself establish that one role caused another role's emotion. This boundary governs new development work without relabelling the frozen Paris pilot or rewriting the proposal.

## Acquisition priorities and stopping rules

1. Audit monthly and source/genre distributions within the fixed interval. Explain unusual counts using independent parent identities, duplicate/version relationships, pagination/batch logs, publication dates and extraction boundaries. Distinguish collection artefacts, legitimate publication concentration, independently supported candidate research checkpoints and unresolved peaks. Do not equalise volume automatically or pre-attribute peaks to climate change.
2. Audit provenance quality with separate dimensions for directness, verification, unresolved/disputed provenance and access. Count original utterances, archival reproductions, secondary reporting and mixed/unknown records with evidence references. An archive may preserve a primary source; a news article or public post is primary evidence of its own discourse. Hashes verify saved bytes, not the truth of a claim. Supplement missing originals, broken mappings or dated provenance where a specified uncertainty warrants it.
3. Exhaustive UK recovery, all 821 AU originals and all US 1988–1993 scanned issues are not prerequisites for a pooled report or the next research stage. Defer the estimated 140 GB US full-issue route unless a specific unresolved research window needs it and bounded retrieval is justified. Archive completion alone does not establish a research need for extra storage.
4. Apply existing bounded retries/cooldowns. Record a blocked route and pursue an alternative only for a specified evidence need. Reopen it for a new viable route, changed access or unresolved research checkpoint, rather than to reconfirm the same obstacle.
5. At a committed checkpoint, publish one pooled coverage snapshot and residual-gap list. Ongoing/blocked country queues do not prevent reporting available evidence. Label it a snapshot rather than final source completion.
6. After a bounded tranche is accounted for, advance usable intervals to structural/numerical cleaning. Prepare later relevance-validation algorithms if useful, but defer substantive climate/emotion assessment and semantic exclusions to analysis. Existing incomplete source queues remain recorded; this priority update neither declares them complete nor instructs agents to restart or expand them automatically.
