# Fear of Temperature — Supervisor Briefing

## What the project now measures

The project now asks when, through which voices, and through which linguistic relations temperature or climate becomes connected to threat/risk/harm and to explicit affect. The analytical object is a validated passage containing an A/B temperature-climate object, with a separately validated relation to D threat or C affect. **Threat is not affect**: risk, crisis, harm, or emergency language does not by itself establish fear, worry, or anxiety.

## Why the keyword stage was necessary

The 180 Priority Candidates created the controlled historical vocabulary, anchor structure, voice categories, source map, lexical families, dictionary checks, Ngram trajectories, and bounded-search audit needed to retrieve passages reproducibly. That work prevents modern terminology, generic word frequency, or easily digitised sources from silently defining the history.

## What the relationship analysis adds

The new layer defines `AB_object_passages`, explicit threat and affect linkage registries, anchor and voice denominators, low-N rules, four affect modes, and a database relation that cannot be created by co-occurrence alone. It also separates four lexicalisation markers: first Ngram nonzero, first sustained Ngram presence, first candidate-level attestation, and first candidate-level target-sense anchor.

## Preliminary findings

- The constructed inventory contains A=47, B=29, C=33, and D=71 candidates. Its voices are unevenly represented: V1=71, V2=49, V3=18, V4=4, and V5=38. These figures describe inventory structure, not historical prevalence.
- Passage-level threat and affect linkage rates are **not yet estimable**. The database has no populated passage → occurrence → annotation → review → linkage chain, so all A/B denominators are zero. Rates are left blank rather than reported as 0%.
- Raw Ngram appearance often precedes the candidate-level target-sense anchor by decades or more. For example, `global warming` first sustains in 1863 but is target-anchored in 1988; `climate anxiety` sustains in 2010 and is target-anchored in 2022. Early raw strings require OCR, adjacency, and sense review.
- Bounded-search support rises from 21/30 candidates in 1842 and 20/30 in 1938 to 29/30 in 2022. This is discoverability bias, not proof that modern language was historically more prevalent.
- The current evidence can show where the lexical and archival record is strong or weak, but it cannot yet establish which voices most often connect temperature/climate to threat or affect.

## Next semantic validation step

The next step is **targeted semantic passage validation guided by the relationship results**: retrieve source-linked A/B passages, preserve source and speaker boundaries, validate each C/D relation in context, retain direct/prescribed/elicited/researcher-labelled affect modes, double-code a quality-control sample, and compute voice comparisons only after denominator and low-N checks pass.
