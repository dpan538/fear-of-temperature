# Learned temperature fear, ML measurement and event dynamics

Decision date: 13 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane. Status: accepted research direction from the user's explicit clarification; empirical hypotheses remain to be tested. This decision supersedes the previous three-RQ operational framing for prospective research. It preserves submitted proposals, historical decisions, frozen data and reports.

The user's macro hypothesis is **“Learn to fear of temperature is a shared cultural heritage.”** A working formulation is **learning to fear temperature as shared cultural heritage**, studied in the Australian climate/temperature discourse corpus. The current empirical case does not itself establish worldwide generality.

The project now has three linked research questions and analysis aims:

1. **RQ1 — Longitudinal learning-like development.** How do broad negative climate/temperature affect, worry/anxiety and Fear vary across publication time? After accounting for source, genre, capture process, text version and event composition, are the patterns consistent with a learned and culturally shared response? Examine possible changes in prevalence, intensity, targets, metaphors, bodily narratives, memory and explicit transmission/learning claims. A monotonic increase is a hypothesis-compatible possibility, not a cleaning target or guaranteed result.
2. **RQ2 — Valid text classification and scoring.** Can ML/DL reliably recognize and classify the spectrum from general negative affect through climate-related distress to Fear, with calibrated intensity and uncertainty? Validate recognition of mild/somatic/indirect/ecological/metaphorical expressions alongside strong Fear, mixed affect, negation, quotation and policy/scientist/alarmism backlash. Assess general-negative intensity and Fear-specific probability/intensity separately; strong anger or grief is not automatically Fear.
3. **RQ3 — Event-centred emotional change.** How do negative affect, Fear, targets and narrative attributions change before, during and after independently evidenced climate/temperature-related events? Link traceable text and model scores to predeclared event windows, retaining overlapping events and distinguishing text activity volume from emotion proportions/intensity. Interpret event-level dynamics together with original passages rather than treating every spike as a causal event effect.

These questions replace the old priority sequence in which RQ1/RQ2 were primarily numerical activity inventories and Fear interpretation was deferred to RQ3. Government/official records remain useful for event timing and contextual evidence; a mandatory three-role government/media/public emotion-series model is not reinstated.

Working empirical hypotheses corresponding to those questions are:

- **H1:** The longitudinal record will contain a learning-compatible development of climate/temperature-negative affect and Fear, together with shared memory, framing or transmission narratives. Test whether apparent progression persists under source, genre, length, event, capture and text-version sensitivities; allow adaptation, reversals and non-monotonic periods rather than forcing every year upward.
- **H2:** An appropriately validated ML/DL measurement model can distinguish and score general negativity, climate-related affect and Fear-specific expression across the continuum, including subtle and indirect cases, with useful out-of-group recognition and calibrated uncertainty. Compare this against transparent lexical/baseline measurements; do not treat query labels as evidence that the model succeeds.
- **H3:** Independently evidenced events will have interpretable, event-specific changes in affect type, intensity, target or narrative over their pre/during/post windows. Some events may show fear increases, others backlash, coping, mixed responses or no detectable change; these remain empirical outcomes.

ML/DL has two responsibilities: a defensible measurement system for text-level affect, and evidence for longitudinal and event-level analysis. Classification performance supports the measurement step; a rising score curve alone does not prove cultural transmission or a causal learning mechanism. Evidence may support, qualify or fail to support the macro hypothesis. Preserve explicit learning/intergenerational narrative evidence and test alternatives such as source turnover, retrospective memory, later edits and changing event exposure.

The intended output is a traceable score vector rather than one keyword-derived binary label: climate/temperature relation, general-negative intensity, affect subtype/mode, Fear probability/intensity, speaker, target, narrative time and uncertainty. Preserve positive, neutral, mild, mixed, medical and indirect texts. Training balance can be adjusted only after gold labels and group partitions exist; source records and later positive/neutral texts are not pruned to manufacture temporal progression.

Repeated expressions, metaphors or narratives across people and times may themselves be relevant to cultural circulation. Keep native record counts, speaker/date lineage and documented sharing/work relationships. Encoding can reuse a text cache and evaluation can group duplicate-risk records, but preprocessing must not replace every occurrence's voice/date with the first copy or erase repetition from the historical evidence.

The current working full-text frame remains the collected Australian 2003–2025 corpus, with the existing 801,559 formal records, body versions and 1,383 reversible current-phase holds preserved. Older publication intervals/background holdings retain their recorded scope. This research decision does not reopen collection, modify native dates, authorize destructive filtering or initiate full-corpus model training/labeling.

The immediate authorized task is a **pre-chunk design audit**: determine derived text-noise filtering, training/validation/test grouping, train-only fitting and context-preserving chunk strategy. Existing demo models and code are audited candidates, not an adopted final ML/DL architecture. See [the audit](NEXT_STEPS.md).

Classification evaluation should test group-disjoint recognition across years, with year/source/event-stratified diagnostics. Publication-time holdout examines temporal transfer; it does not certify historical as-of forecasting from later captured text. Event-held-out evaluation is a separate test of unseen-event generalization. Every experiment freezes work/version/duplicate/thread/context groups before chunk generation; all child chunks inherit their parent's split.

For a later longitudinal analysis, report record-level class proportions and calibrated intensity with source/genre/time/capture sensitivities and year-specific error checks. For event analysis, freeze event identity, timing, comparison windows and independent count units before examining the emotion response; retain narrative-event time separately from publication time. These are prospective analysis commitments, not completed empirical findings.
