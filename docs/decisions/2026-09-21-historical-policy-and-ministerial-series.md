## Decision

- Add historical environmental/climate/energy policy material from relevant UK departments and verified predecessor bodies.
- Admit ministerial answers and statements as a distinct government-discourse series. Preserve question/context text separately from government responses. Opposition contributions, questioners and unidentified speakers are not automatically government voice.
- Keep policy papers, ministerial answers and ministerial statements separately identified and counted. Do not splice pre-2010 ministerial speech into post-2010 policy-paper counts as a single homogeneous series.
- Use the same 1988-01-01 to 2026-09-21 study boundary for discovery of the ministerial series, with pre-2010 work first. Actual access/coverage determines available intervals; do not claim continuity until enumerated.
- The implementation handoff specifies a bounded first acquisition tranche: pre-2010 related-department policies and Commons ministerial written answers/statements within the relevant departmental remit. Commons is an implementation boundary, not a claim to cover both Houses or all ministerial communication. Broader oral debates, Lords and other countries are deferred.
- Use the existing authoritative 06 database. Register only genuinely new source records; attach alternate locations or versions to existing identities when evidence supports a match. Keep frozen 04/05 artefacts intact.
- Preserve the existing separation between project collection authorization and formal institutional ethics status. This approval does not fabricate HREC approval or extend redistribution rights.
- No proposal rewrite, model evaluation, vector processing or repeated approval gates. Record changes, counts and exceptions; conduct one focused final acceptance.

## Count and denominator boundaries

Expected counts must come from enumerated, deduplicated manifests for each source/year/genre. Distinguish an evolving discovery count from a frozen target. Topic-selected records cannot supply a denominator for all government communication; counts without demonstrable enumeration completeness remain observed counts.
