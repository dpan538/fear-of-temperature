# Global temporal coverage and macro feedback

Date: 27 September 2026
Status: user-confirmed research direction; pooling and weighting remain methodological design choices.

Implementation: [PROJECT_DIRECTION.md](../PROJECT_DIRECTION.md) now governs project-wide acquisition priorities, reporting and stopping rules.

## User clarification

The primary objective is to assemble observations across regions and contemporaneous events to study macro-level responses to climate and warming. Temporal gap recovery therefore concerns the international corpus, not solely the restoration of a UK institutional series. The project must not be restricted in advance to regional findings. Jurisdiction is a retained source attribute and potential adjustment variable, rather than the compulsory boundary of the primary conclusions.

## Further user clarification: evidence across time

The user places the study within a shared collective knowledge, memory and culture perspective. Acquisition should prioritise authentic event and documentary evidence across the study's time intervals and checkpoints. Exhaustive recovery within a single jurisdiction is not the completion criterion. Shared memory/culture provides an interpretive perspective; the existence of a pooled corpus does not itself demonstrate globally shared awareness, emotion or cultural status.

Report temporal evidence using the interval/checkpoint, independently documented event or publication activity, dated primary-text parents, represented discourse roles, and remaining evidence gaps. Distinguish an event being documented from evidence that government, media or public actors responded to it. An unrelated official record does not establish climate-event or fear-response coverage.

Event coverage guides gap-recovery priorities rather than replacing the sampling frame. Preserve routine and low-attention periods and the existing eligible-document denominators for attention measurement. Do not select events or periods because they display strong fear, or invent an event to populate an empty month. Retain unresolved or genuinely quiet intervals explicitly. Event-linked and background observations together support the temporal study.

## Implications for acquisition and reporting

- US, EU, UK and Australian records can contribute to the same calendar month's international government corpus. A new jurisdiction can fill an empty pooled month or strengthen an already observed month. Both contributions should be reported explicitly.
- Reassess US acquisition by the additional usable months, event-window coverage and distinct relevant parent records it provides to the international corpus. Describing it only as geographic enrichment understates the user's intended purpose.
- Keep the within-source coverage ledger alongside the pooled monthly view. A US contribution can close a pooled observation gap while a UK archive gap remains unresolved; these two statements are compatible.
- Government, media and public roles retain separate time series for RQ1. Government observations alone do not establish a complete three-role comparison window.
- Contemporary events retain their own identities, dates, locations and types. Texts from the same month may be aggregated as observations, but temporal coincidence does not establish a shared event or common cause.
- Count independent publication/response parents. Chunks, attachments and mirrored or versioned copies do not inflate the number of independent observations.

## Interpretation and analysis design

Continuous monthly observations support a statement about temporal presence in the collected international English-language corpus. They do not alone establish complete global historical coverage, population representativeness, a fear response, or a causal effect. Relevance and measurement validity remain to be assessed.

The primary analysis may examine pooled cross-regional attention, fear-related expression and temporal responses. Source, genre and jurisdiction composition must remain visible so that a new data source entering the corpus is not automatically interpreted as an increase in fear. A documented pooling/weighting rule and sensitivity comparison using stable contributors should be chosen during measurement design. These are safeguards for the global research objective, not a decision to make regional comparison the principal result.

For RQ2, assess the same prespecified event windows across available regions and roles, preserving overlapping-event and changing-source indicators. Geographic scope of the conclusions should follow the evidence; the project does not preclude a macro-level finding.

This clarifies the 26 September coverage decision and corrects the coordinator's overly country-specific interpretation of gap recovery. Existing source identities, raw records and acquisition authorisations remain applicable.
