## Unit and frame before comparison

The main count is one **source-specific independent document parent** (`documents.document_id`) or one EU CELLAR **Work URI**, grouped by publication month, source and genre. It is not a cross-source deduplicated utterance count. A UK parliamentary answer can link to a shared source page; a policy paper can have several attachments; a saved page can yield many extraction segments. These units are separate in [unit_and_duplicate_diagnostics.csv](unit_and_duplicate_diagnostics.csv) and [Figure 2](figures/figure_02_count_units.png). The full 465-month grid, source-span flag, two unknown-date rows and partial-month marker are in [monthly_source_genre_counts.csv](monthly_source_genre_counts.csv). Its extracted-status field is an operational status count, **not** fresh verification of readable historical text: UK DEFRA parent status is stale relative to saved content, and some EU extraction statuses still require layout or placeholder review.

## Temporal concentration and anomalies

[Figure 1](figures/figure_01_monthly_intensity.png) shows within-frame monthly intensity. The largest **absolute** months are not automatically unusual under a seasonal within-source reference: UK historic answers had 1,761 in 2000-11 (robust score 1.83), modern UK answers 1,154 in 2025-03 (1.33), and EU COM 346 in 2002-09 (1.75). Each has distinct parent/Work identifiers in the saved frame. [absolute_peak_context.csv](absolute_peak_context.csv) retains their top days, linked-object counts and batch evidence; none is flattened. The US final-rule maximum, 110 in 1996-02, *does* meet the review rule but still has 110 distinct IDs, URLs, objects and versions. No count peak is assigned to a climate event.

The reproducible rule in [build_distribution.py](build_distribution.py) compares a month with **positive-count same-calendar months within five years** in the same source × genre, requiring at least four peers and excluding partial September 2026. It uses `log1p` median/MAD with a 0.35 floor; a review flag requires absolute score ≥2.5 plus a twofold and ≥10-parent excess for highs, or at most half a baseline of ≥10 for lows. Targeted UK mirror and small/unverified AU frames have no continuous denominator and are not scored. There are **85** flags in the full [score ledger](anomaly_scores_all_months.csv). A deterministic selection reserves the top high per source, then ranks by score with source, direction and month caps; [anomaly_ledger.csv](anomaly_ledger.csv) details **20** distinct review flags. Four near-baseline months in [ordinary_comparison_periods.csv](ordinary_comparison_periods.csv) show what an ordinary period looks like. Flags nominate checks and never exclude records.

**Calendar-supported lows.** No selected Commons answer in 2024-06 is consistent with the [30 May dissolution and 9 July return](https://www.parliament.uk/about/how/elections-and-voting/general/general-election-2024-timetable/). The 2015-04/05 zeros align with the [30 March dissolution and 18 May reassembly](https://www.parliament.uk/business/news/news-by-year/2015/march/prorogation-end-of-session/); 1997-04 aligns with the [8 April dissolution](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld199899/ldselect/ldprivi/106i/106i20.htm); 2010-05 follows the [12 April dissolution and 18 May return](https://www.parliament.uk/business/news/news-by-year/2010/04/dissolution-of-parliament-12-april-2010/). The [1999](https://www.parliament.uk/about/faqs/house-of-commons-faqs/business-faq-page/recess-dates/list-of-previous-commons-recess-dates/) and [2000](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm200001/csession/100/10010.htm) September zeros align with Commons summer recesses. These independent calendar dates explain likely source availability, not all-government absence or every API record. UK 2005-05 has only four selected answer dates (23–26 May), so its 54 parents should not be treated as a full ordinary month.

**Structural/collection findings.** In 2013-05 the official UK archive has 62 selected answers on **two dates and two shared pages**; the annual query partitions explicitly say `partial`. The targeted mirror has another 120 records on six dates, but the two sources cannot simply be summed as independent utterances. In 2013-04 DEFRA has 33 distinct policy parents, 25 dated 9 April, including 23 titles in a `2010 to 2015 government policy` series. The 33 parents link to 73 objects, so attachments do not explain the parent spike. Many saved pages show 2015 update times; current saved body wording cannot be presumed identical to its 2013 state. In 2011-06 DEFRA has 17 parents and 35 objects, with five distinct papers dated 14 June. These are dated publication clusters, not established climate-event responses. Bounded IDs and object relationships are in [bounded_parent_object_samples.csv](bounded_parent_object_samples.csv).

**Unresolved flags.** The selected US metadata has 1994 dates only in January, September and November, leaving the March/May/June zero flags despite reconciled pages in its API selection. This establishes a **selected-frame gap**, not absence of EPA/DOE rules in the Federal Register; the separate pre-1994 scanned route is irrelevant to these 1994 bins. EU COM has 186 distinct Works in 1988-04, 67 dated 5 April and 136 with staged Items; a real release batch versus historical date assignment remains unresolved. The 2024-04, 2024-11 and 2026-04 EU lows are confined to the frozen COM/preparatory class and have no staged Items; they do not estimate all Commission proposal output. UK API 2024-08 has 62 distinct answer IDs on 1–5 August, while the [Commons summer recess began 30 July](https://www.parliament.uk/business/news/2024/july/the-house-of-commons-rises-for-summer-recess/); a bounded original-page date check is needed before interpreting this high. The US 1996-02 high of 110 remains an unassigned selected-frame concentration.

## Long text and extraction splitting

The existing [UK record-level length summary](long_text_segment_evidence.csv), dated 22 September, gives written-answer median **513 characters and 2 segments** (99th percentile 3,918 characters and 10 segments; 235,420 summarized records). Policy-document median is **46,996 characters and 672 segments** (99th percentile about 1.67 million characters and 37,233 segments; 1,054 summarized documents). This report is a dated summary with its own inclusion rule and is not arithmetically joined to the source/genre parent counts above. The separate 21 September [format/segment summary](../../06_government_content_acquisition/text_quality_review/03_segment_length_distribution.csv) has **1,362 PDF objects → 2,745,617 PDF line/block segments**, median **38 characters** per segment. One bounded DEFRA April 2013 paper links a PDF with 18,983 segments; another links a PDF with 4,099. Two May 2013 archive pages link 62 answer parents. Thus long files and extraction splitting can vastly inflate a **segment** total, while the parent-month ledger remains at 33 or 62 respectively. Do not use raw segment counts as independent document volume or bulk resegment to smooth peaks.

## Research boundary and action

This audit establishes **Level 1 only**: source-framed dated parent/Work presence and operational saved-text stages. **Level 2** climate/warming relevance and similarity, **Level 3** affect/risk/future-harm/responsibility association, and **Level 4** fear-specific interpretation were **not assessed**. Neither the 465/465 recorded pooled government text-month milestone nor these counts establishes climate relevance, emotion prevalence, a comparable three-role series, or causal influence. Neutral and low-volume periods remain in frame.

The short [ranked repair and annotation list](REPAIR_LIST.md) separates a targeted structural/date check from source-calendar annotations and conditional future supplementation. No count was deleted, downsampled, semantically filtered or retrospectively reclassified. Figures and validation scope are recorded in [FIGURE_NOTES.md](FIGURE_NOTES.md).
