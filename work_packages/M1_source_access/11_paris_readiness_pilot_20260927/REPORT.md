## 1. What the 49 × 3 table establishes

The [147-row readiness table](paris_month_role_readiness_49x3.csv) has one independent month-role row for every bin. The [field dictionary](FIELD_DICTIONARY.md) specifies every denominator and blank/zero distinction. Its government row retains UK, EU, US and AU provenance rather than treating unlike genres as one rate. The documented checkpoint lower bounds across the window are 23,725 dated source parents and 13,751 readable source parents, with some source updates after that checkpoint not counted. These numbers are inventory lower bounds, **not** comparable climate or fear observations. All 49 government months have at least one readable parent according to the later [bridge ledger](../09_us_au_government_acquisition/reports/targeted_2015_bridge/updated_monthly_government_presence.csv); full-month warming relevance remains unreviewed in every bin.

The frozen EPA `final_rule` series supplies a genuine same-rule eligible-document denominator: **2,304 distinct EPA parents in all 49 months**. It has **125 acquired readable originals in 10 months**, including a census of 63 in April 2015 and 38 in May 2015, plus three selected originals in each of eight other months. The other **39 EPA months are metadata-only** for that series. The [fixed rule](../09_us_au_government_acquisition/reports/targeted_2015_bridge/SCOPE.md), [monthly source table](../09_us_au_government_acquisition/reports/targeted_2015_bridge/monthly_source_coverage_2012_2019.csv), and [parent acceptance ledger](../09_us_au_government_acquisition/reports/targeted_2015_bridge/parent_acceptance.csv) support those counts. Government pooled presence must not be read as EPA continuity or as warming relevance.

For media, the only audited frame is one Guardian environment archive **day** in each of November 2015, December 2015 and January 2016. Those pages held **22, 17 and 7** distinct nonvideo title-card URLs. Four selected article parents had original publication timestamps and readable body HTML. The other 46 months have no audited media corpus here. The single-day counts are not whole-month denominators, and the archive pages may have changed since 2015–16. Guardian Open Platform currently requires an API key; a test-key request returned HTTP 401, so no API-wide denominator or full-text bulk access is claimed ([official access page](https://open-platform.theguardian.com/access/)). The [archive-day frame](media_day_archive_frame.csv) and [selected article checks](media_pilot_review.csv) make this limited access observable.

For the public role, the official [2015–2017 UK Parliament archived-petition search](https://petition.parliament.uk/archived/petitions?parliament=1) exposes dated IDs and petitioner-authored action/background text. At this snapshot the bounded `parliament=1&q=climate` JSON search yielded **179 result positions over 8 pages, 177 distinct petition IDs**: IDs 155275 and 178721 each appeared on two pages. All 177 had nonempty petitioner background/additional-detail text, and their creation dates span July 2015–April 2017. The matrix reports 20 months with one or more readable query hits and two **verified zero months within this exact keyword query** during July 2015–April 2017; its other 27 public months remain outside the audited query frame. A search hit is neither a climate-relevant petition nor a published petition. The [query frame](public_climate_query_frame.csv), [month counts](public_query_month_counts.csv), and [near-event 21-parent subset](public_pilot_review.csv) retain `created_at`, `opened_at`, rejected state and the original JSON URL. The archive page itself lists 31,730 submissions and 10,950 published petitions for this Parliament; our 177 is the narrower query frame, not that archive-wide denominator ([archive all](https://petition.parliament.uk/archived/petitions?parliament=1), [published subset](https://petition.parliament.uk/archived/petitions?parliament=1&state=published)).

## 2. Government cleaning and topical review

The [prespecified selection](SCOPE.md) uses seed `paris-readiness-20260927-v1`, four nearby months for UK answers/policy and EPA (2015-02/03/06/07 for UK; 2015-03/04/05/06 for EPA), one hash-selected broad title candidate and one hash-selected control where available. An explicitly documented post hoc exact-title supplement checks obvious climate candidates because the first broad `energy` sample contained many false positives. The [selected ledger](government_pilot_selected.csv) and [manual review](government_pilot_review.csv) contain **23 distinct parent records**: 3 UK policy parents, 11 UK ministerial answer parents and 9 EPA original rules. The UK has no stored parent from these two selected strata in April or May 2015, so those bins are not silently filled with a UK answer. EPA supplies the pooled source-text bridge there.

For each UK policy parent, the landing page and linked attachment stay attached to **one** parent. The February anaerobic-digestion report has a PDF attachment with 709 source segments and 47,721 extracted characters, while its landing page has 11 segments and 770 characters; the selected passage comes from the report, not an invented second publication. The March smarter-data report also has a linked PDF (638 segments), but the inspected carbon mention is about information requests, not warming fear. The July carrier-bag page has a linked HTML attachment and a later current-version timestamp in 2021; its July 2015 bin uses the first publication date, but current wording cannot be assumed to match that first version. PDF line breaks, duplicated web titles, licence boilerplate and attachment dominance remain cleaning issues. Source locators and version IDs are retained in the selected ledger.

For ministerial questions, the content version can represent a shared API JSON page, while a parent is the **individual written question/answer**. The review used `source_id=question:<id>` and `source_id=answer:<id>:<n>` locators to keep question context separate from the government response. Two exact-title `Climate Change` answers discuss a carbon budget or climate lobbying and are topical; a `Department for Energy and Climate Change: Off-payroll Working` answer is an obvious title-based false positive. Other `Energy`/`Air Pollution` answers address tariffs, operating durations, local air quality or departmental administration. The inspected segment IDs map to the selected parent content versions; an answer-page segment count is never an answer count.

The EPA originals use the accepted Federal Register issue date, document number, EPA heading, official rules section, raw SHA-256 and content-version identity in the [bridge acceptance](../09_us_au_government_acquisition/reports/targeted_2015_bridge/REPORT.md). The nine diagnostic parents include **six ordinary final rules/amendments, two CFR corrections and one direct final rule**. The April greenhouse-gas-reporting hit is a short CFR correction, not a fresh climate argument. The May greenhouse-gas permitting hit is a direct final rule about permit rescission after litigation. Generic air-pollution rules, pesticide tolerances and an `Energy Supply` boilerplate clause were false positives for warming fear. The original HTML/pre wrapper, rule headers and regulatory boilerplate must be stripped or retained as metadata before passages are used. The bridge previously checked that all 24,313 cleaned segments in its 125-parent tranche mapped to the correct source versions; this pilot did not rerun a global segment audit.

**Finite review result:** 5/23 government samples had a climate/warming topic connection, including administrative mitigation/regulatory context; 0/23 contained a verified explicit fear expression. These are labels on an enriched diagnostic sample, not a government-month rate or an estimate of fear prevalence. The matrix stores these counts only in `pilot_*` columns for sampled months; full-month relevance cells stay blank.

## 3. Media and public feasibility checks

The four Guardian article samples comprise a wildlife negative control, a pre-agreement climate-summit story, a post-agreement energy-policy story, and a later Heathrow climate-activist trial story. **Three are climate-topic relevant; none contains an explicit fear expression in the reviewed passages.** The records preserve the original publication timestamp, the later modification timestamp, archive-day URL, current body presence and response SHA-256. Current HTML body availability proves a feasible bounded capture route, not historical version fidelity. Only short passages are stored; complete Guardian article bodies were not redistributed. Article and archive URLs are in the [media review](media_pilot_review.csv).

The 21 UK petition search hits created in November 2015–January 2016 contain **13 published petitions and 8 rejected submissions**. All have independent IDs, creation timestamps and readable petitioner text; rejected text must not be treated as publicly visible participation at creation. Manual petitioner-only review found **14/21 climate-topic records** and **6/21 with anticipated climate-harm cues** (for example coastal loss or flood damage). It found **0/21 explicit fear expressions** under the narrow pilot rule. The rejected geoengineering/“chemtrails” item and figurative uses of “climate” show why query hits require review. Government responses embedded in some petition JSON were excluded from the public voice. A creation-date series and an `opened_at` published-only series would differ; signatures are a later cumulative attribute and are not dated individual utterances. The [public review](public_pilot_review.csv) records each decision and exclusion.

The official petition site's [Open Government Licence notice](https://petition.parliament.uk/archived/petitions?parliament=1) supports saving this small source pilot with attribution and caveats. This petition forum is a narrow, moderated public channel, not a representative sample of general public opinion. Guardian's [Open Platform terms](https://www.theguardian.com/open-platform/terms-and-conditions) and current article access require a separate rights/access decision before any large full-text media corpus is built.

## 4. Minimum comparable interval and next priority

**November 2015–January 2016 is the smallest observed three-role pilot interval**: government source text is present in each month, the fixed Guardian archive-day probes yield dated body-bearing articles in each, and the petition query yields 4, 14 and 3 creation-dated independent submissions respectively. This is a **feasibility overlap**, not a comparable monthly panel. Guardian contributes one day per month; petitions are a moderated keyword-search frame with rejected/published states; government sources and genres change and EPA is not complete in those months. Therefore no lead/lag ordering, event response or fear trend can be inferred.

Next, freeze a genuinely monthly media article frame and body-access rule for the candidate three-month overlap, then extend it across the 49-month window only if the source holds up. An authorised Guardian API key with an explicit monthly query/result denominator is one route; systematic day-archive auditing is the minimum keyless alternative, with archive completeness and current-version limits recorded. In parallel, freeze a public inclusion rule (published only versus all submitted petitions), obtain its monthly eligible-petition denominator rather than only `q=climate` hits, and check continuity across the 2015/2017 Parliament boundaries. Expand source-aware government passage cleaning within the same genres only after date/version, attachment and regulatory-subtype rules are settled. A later role/time analysis should use validated relevant **parent** counts or shares within each defensible source frame, source-mix sensitivity checks, and independent event dates. RQ3 stays deferred.
